The problem with printing today

Students and workers need printed documents often, but getting them printed costs time and money.

Travel

Customers must go to a printing shop, even for a few pages.

Waiting

Shops get crowded during busy school periods.

Cost

Some customers find current printing prices expensive.

How it works

A simple seven-step process, from file to finished document.

  1. Customer sends the file digitally.
  2. Customer picks black-and-white or colored.
  3. Customer gives the number of pages or copies.
  4. We calculate the total cost.
  5. The document is printed.
  6. Customer chooses pickup or delivery.
  7. Delivery adds a ₱15 fee.

Risk analysis

The biggest risks are competition, operating costs, and customer demand.

Final decision

Feasible with conditions

Respondents print often and accept the prices, but the estimated profit of ₱763 per month is low. The business must grow its orders and control its costs.

Regular demand69.7% print several times a week or every day.
Strong price acceptance84.8% accept ₱1 B&W and 90.9% accept ₱2 colored.
Delivery has potential75.8% will pay the ₱15 fee.

Conditions for success

  • Increase the number of daily orders.
  • Control paper and ink expenses.
  • Maintain the printer properly.
  • Promote the business to students and nearby customers.
  • Keep prices affordable.
  • Track actual sales and expenses.
  • Consider additional services later.

Recommendations

  1. Start with a small operation to limit financial risk.
  2. Focus marketing on students and school communities.
  3. Promote the ₱1 B&W and ₱2 colored prices.
  4. Offer ₱15 delivery as an option.
  5. Accept online file submissions.
  6. Compare paper and ink suppliers.
  7. Track daily sales and expenses.
  8. Monitor customer feedback.
  9. Add photocopying, scanning, and other document services.
  10. Run a new survey after launch to get reliable demand data.
